Pause Before You Click

Phishing is a trick used to get you to share information, send money, or open a harmful link or attachment. These messages can arrive through email, text messages, or social media.

Look for Warning Signs

  • A message pressures you to act immediately.
  • You are asked to share a password or verification code.
  • A sender asks for an unexpected payment.
  • A link or sender address does not match the organization.
  • An attachment arrives when you were not expecting it.

A message can have correct spelling and a familiar logo and still be a scam.

Example Message

“Your account will close today. Click here and enter your password to keep it open.”

The urgent deadline and request for your password are warning signs. Open the organization's app or type its known website address yourself to check your account.

What Should You Do?

  1. Avoid clicking links or opening attachments in a suspicious message.
  2. Contact the organization using a phone number or website you already trust.
  3. Report the message using your email provider's phishing report option.
  4. Delete the message after reporting it.

If You Already Responded

If you shared a password, change it through the real website and update any accounts that reused it. Contact your bank if you shared payment details. At work or school, tell the IT support team.
